# Standard: Research Integrity & Strategic Rigor

---

## 1. Principles of Verification
1. **Source Citation:** All factual claims, market size figures, demographic trends, and competitor revenue benchmarks must cite their foundation.
2. **Estimation Transparency:** When actual figures are not publicly disclosed, use bottom-up estimation frameworks and state the assumptions explicitly:
   $$\text{Market Potential} = \text{Target Population} \times \text{Smartphone Penetration} \times \text{Ride Frequency} \times \text{Average Fare}$$
3. **No Phantom Metrics:** Never invent percentages, conversion rates, or customer survey responses. Label estimates clearly as `[Modeled Projection]` or `[Empirical Benchmark]`.

## 2. Competitive Analysis Requirements
Every competitor analysis (e.g. Bolt, Uber, inDrive vs. Little Ride) must evaluate:
- **Price Architecture:** Base fare, per-kilometer rate, waiting fees, commission rates.
- **Service Categories:** Multimodal offerings (Boda, Bajaj, Standard, Premium/Comfort, Delivery).
- **Safety Features:** In-app SOS, ride sharing, driver vetting rigor, gender-specific options.
- **Payment Infrastructure:** Mobile money (M-Pesa, Airtel Money, Tigo Pesa), cards, cash, corporate billing.

## 3. Financial & Budget Standards
- Campaign budgets must include clear line-item allocations: Creative Production, Paid Performance, Influencer Engagements, Local Activation, and Contingency.
- Projected CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) and LTV (Lifetime Value) must align with regional benchmarks (e.g., Sub-Saharan Africa ride-hailing CAC typically $1.50 – $3.50 per verified first-ride user).
